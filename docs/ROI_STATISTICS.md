# Per-plane ROI statistics (algorithm roi-1)

The image viewer has independent line, circular and polygonal drawing tools. Circles are defined by center and radius in XY pixel coordinates. Polygons must be simple, with distinct vertices; pixel centers on boundaries are included. Small regions containing fewer than three pixel centers are rejected. Circles extending beyond the image are clipped with a warning. The ROI's recorded Z index selects its native source plane, independently of the currently displayed plane. No max projection, display contrast adjustment or smoothing enters area statistics.

For each pair A/B within each ROI:

- Pearson r is the centered normalized covariance; Spearman rho is Pearson correlation of average ranks (ties included). Constant channels yield unavailable correlation.
- Manders overlap coefficient is sum(A B) / sqrt(sum(A²) sum(B²)). A zero norm yields unavailable overlap.
- Raw M1 = sum(A where B > 0) / sum(A); raw M2 reverses A/B. These are intensity fractions, not probabilities of molecular interaction.
- Thresholded M1 = sum(A where B > threshold B) / sum(A), with M2 reversed. The denominator includes the entire ROI. A zero denominator yields unavailable values. Comparisons are strictly greater than the threshold.
- Threshold methods are zero, manually supplied per-channel native intensities, or a Costes-style search using positive total-least-squares regression. The automatic method searches downward over the higher-variance channel's integer range, retaining the highest valid threshold with nonpositive correlation among pixels below either paired threshold. The other threshold is continuous along the regression line. At least three background pixels and nonzero variance in both channels are required. Nonpositive covariance, constant channels, or failure to find such a threshold yield unavailable thresholded measurements. There is no mean-threshold fallback. Continuous secondary thresholds differ from software that rounds both thresholds to native integers; exact Fiji parity is not claimed.

Regression standardizes each nonconstant channel within the ROI, uses the selected target channel as response, and fits the other channels by ordinary least squares. This is also the coefficient MLE under an assumed independent, equal-variance Gaussian residual model. R² is descriptive. Signed standardized coefficients are preserved. Rank-deficient or ill-conditioned candidate channels suppress individual coefficients and their ratio. The coefficient ratio is the largest divided by second-largest absolute coefficient, when defined. No causal interpretation or uncertainty calibration is supplied.

The optional Gaussian likelihood-ratio statistic is -N log(1-r²), comparing unrestricted bivariate Gaussian covariance with zero covariance under an independent-pixel model. Perfect correlation is a singular boundary and is reported unavailable. It has no calibrated p-value here: neighboring pixels are dependent and the acquisition noise model is not established. This statistic is not Bayesian evidence, a probability of binding, or a probability that a knockdown causes a phenotype. These inference workflows require separate models and validation.

All channel pairs (six for four channels) are reported. Pixel counts, calibrated pixel area, means, population standard deviations, intensity sums, coordinates, source hash, metadata, threshold settings and algorithm version accompany the export. Successful calculations are saved locally. Reinspection invalidates saved results when image metadata/settings change. ROIs are separate XY masks; they are not volumetric objects or biological replicates. Biological comparisons, spatial randomization, registration uncertainty, background correction and denoising are separate development work.

The implementation is independently written from standard mathematical definitions. Validation includes analytic examples and an independent brute-force threshold search. Cross-software and real-experiment validation remain outstanding.
